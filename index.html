Regulators

Growers boost or block hormones with sprays, drenches and storage treatments. Each card follows the chain from product to hormone to what you see on the tree or fruit, and says how strong the evidence is.

Boosters and blockers

For each hormone: products that boost it, products that block it, and the plant hormone that naturally works against it.

  • GibberellinBoost: GA₃ sprays (fruit set, delaying flowering, slowing peel ageing)Block: paclobutrazol, uniconazole, prohexadione-calcium stop its production (forcing flowering, curbing flush)Natural opponent: ABA (rest and flowering vs growth)
  • EthyleneBoost: ethephon, ethylene gas (pineapple forcing, ripening, degreening)Block: 1-MCP stops the plant sensing it; AVG stops its production; permanganate absorbs it from the air (shelf life, less drop)Natural opponent: auxin (keeps young fruit attached)
  • AuxinBoost: NAA, 2,4-D, 4-CPA (less fruit drop, bigger fruit)Block: no commercial blocker (TIBA is research only)Natural opponent: ethylene (lowers auxin flow and causes drop); cytokinin (side-bud growth)
  • CytokininBoost: CPPU, 6-BA (fruit set and size); KNO₃ acts partly through cytokininBlock: no commercial blockerNatural opponent: auxin at the shoot tip
  • ABABoost: S-ABA (peel colour); water stress and girdling raise itBlock: no commercial blocker (fluridone is research only)Natural opponent: gibberellin (growth vs rest)
  • OthersBoost: MeJA, salicylic acid, brassinolide, putrescine, melatonin (colour, chilling and browning control, fruit set)Block: no commercial blockersNatural opponent: salicylic acid and jasmonates often oppose each other; polyamines compete with ethylene for the same building block

Block is a product that stops the plant making or sensing a hormone. Natural opponent is another of the plant's own hormones with the opposite effect; where no blocker exists, growers shift the balance through it.

How long regulators stay in soil

Longest-lasting first. Times are approximate: about five soil half-lives, when roughly 97% has broken down. Real times vary with soil type, temperature, moisture and repeated use. Tap a product for measured orchard residues and effects.

Cause and effect for your crop

Not dosing advice. Rates and timings shown are what each study used, in its own country and conditions. They may not be tested where you farm, and a product may not be registered or legal for your crop. Always follow your local registration and the product label, and ask your extension officer.

Ripening

Climacteric fruits (mango, durian, papaya, passion fruit, avocado) show an ethylene peak after harvest. Non-climacteric fruits such as rambutan stay low.
